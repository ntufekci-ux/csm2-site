How a sovereign-backed buyer credit works, step by step

Public debt funds the EPC contract, the exporter is paid as the work progresses, and an export credit agency carries the lenders’ credit risk.

In brief
  • In a buyer credit, a bank lends to the buyer of exports and an export credit agency guarantees or insures the loan.
  • For public projects, the borrower is typically the ministry of finance or a public entity with a sovereign guarantee.
  • The exporter is paid against documented progress, while the borrower repays over the long term.

Many public infrastructure projects are built under a buyer credit. The contractor delivers the project, a bank lends the money to the public buyer, and an export credit agency covers the bank against non-payment. The structure separates the construction contract from the long-term debt, while keeping both tied to the same project.

The parties

  • Ministry of finance. Borrows directly, or guarantees the debt of a public borrower. If the ministry borrows directly, the facility is a direct sovereign obligation.
  • Public borrower. The project owner or a designated state entity, which signs the loan agreement and repays the lenders.
  • Lending banks. Arrange and fund the buyer credit facility.
  • Export credit agency. Guarantees or insures the loan against political and commercial non-payment risk.
  • EPC contractor or exporter. Designs, supplies and builds under the EPC contract, from the agency’s country.

The numbers

UK Export Finance’s buyer credit facility is a typical example.

85%Maximum share of the contract value that can be lent under the facility
15%Minimum share of the contract value paid by the buyer directly to the exporter
2+ yrsMinimum repayment period, at fixed or floating rates

How the money moves

  • The public owner and the contractor agree the EPC contract, with a price breakdown and payment milestones.
  • The ministry of finance approves the borrowing or issues the sovereign guarantee.
  • The banks and the agency complete due diligence, then sign the loan agreement and the guarantee or insurance.
  • The buyer pays the down payment to the exporter.
  • As the work progresses, the exporter submits certified invoices and milestone evidence and is paid from the facility. UK Export Finance describes the exporter as being paid as though it had a cash contract.
  • After disbursement ends, the borrower repays principal and interest to the lenders over the agreed term.

An example

For the Ministerial City in Cotonou, UK Export Finance supported the Government of Benin, represented by its Ministry of Economy and Finance, with a hybrid direct lending and buyer credit facility of about €121.2 million. Deutsche Bank acted as export credit agent bank, and a joint venture of Arabian Construction Company’s Benin and UK entities was the exporter.

What makes it bankable

The structure works only when every party gets what it needs. The agency needs eligible export content and acceptable country risk. The banks need cover they can rely on. The ministry needs a project that fits its budget and debt plans. The contractor needs a payment route that works during construction. The down payment, local costs and the timing of the sovereign approval are the items that most often hold up close.

A buyer credit separates who builds the project from who carries the long-term debt, without separating either from the project.
