Financial close is not the finish line

Signing the finance documents starts a second project: getting the facility drawn, on time, against documents that withstand scrutiny.

In brief
  • A signed facility is a right to borrow, subject to conditions. It is not cash.
  • Each drawdown is checked against the contract, the cover and the facility agreement.
  • The disbursement procedure should be designed before signing, with the contractor in the room.

Financial close is celebrated as the end of a long process, and for the people who negotiated the term sheet it is. For the project it is the point at which the financing has to start working. Between signing and the last disbursement lie conditions precedent, a long series of drawdown requests and a reporting calendar that runs for the life of the loan.

Why drawdowns stall

In an ECA-backed facility each disbursement is tied to evidence: that goods were shipped or work performed, that the amount claimed is eligible, and that the borrower remains in compliance. The checks are not difficult. They are unforgiving of inconsistency.

  • Invoices that do not follow the price breakdown in the contract.
  • Certificates of origin or shipping documents that are missing, late or issued by the wrong party.
  • Local-cost claims that exceed the supported limit.
  • Milestone certificates that do not match the payment schedule in the facility agreement.
  • Variations to scope that change the eligible content without the lenders being told.
  • Conditions precedent treated as a one-off exercise when several are continuing obligations.

Each of these delays payment to the contractor. A contractor who is not paid slows down, and a delayed project runs up against the availability period of the facility. Problems in the back office reach the construction site quickly.

A facility that cannot be drawn is not financing.

What to settle before signing

Most of this can be prevented at the structuring stage, while the documents are still in draft.

  • Agree the disbursement procedure with the lenders, the agency and the contractor, and test it against a sample invoice.
  • Align the contract price breakdown with the categories the facility recognises: export content, local costs and ineligible items.
  • Decide who prepares, who checks and who signs each document in a drawdown file.
  • Build one tracker for conditions precedent, covenants and reporting dates, and give it an owner.
  • Define how variations will be assessed for their effect on eligibility before they are approved.

None of this is sophisticated. It is the difference between a financing structure that exists on paper and one that pays the contractor on the day the contract says it should.
