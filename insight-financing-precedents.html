Six landmark financings and what they teach

Different countries, sectors and structures, and one common thread: financing closes when the delivery model and the money are designed together.

In brief
  • Each of these projects combined export credit or agency support with a clear borrower, revenue source and contract structure.
  • Award routes differ, from concessions to public contracts and private developments. The financing lessons are common.
  • Figures are as announced at the time. They show structures, not templates.

1915 Çanakkale Bridge and Motorway, Türkiye

On March 16, 2018, the project company signed a financing package of €2.265 billion: a 15-year loan with a five-year grace period from 25 financial institutions in 10 countries, in eight tranches that included export credit agency and Islamic financing. About 70% came from foreign institutions. Korea Eximbank and K-SURE together provided €1 billion in loans and guarantees.

Lesson: very large packages close by combining many lenders and agencies, each sized to what it can carry.

Karapınar Solar, Türkiye

Kalyon’s 1.35 GW Karapınar solar plant reached financial close in November 2021. UK Export Finance guaranteed a US$291 million buyer credit from J.P. Morgan, at the time its largest guarantee for a solar project, linked to GE’s inverter technology. Six Turkish banks provided about US$500 million alongside it.

Lesson: one export package can anchor agency support within a much larger financing that also uses local bank debt.

NEOM Green Hydrogen, Saudi Arabia

NEOM Green Hydrogen Company, an equal joint venture of ACWA Power, Air Products and NEOM, completed financial close in May 2023 at a total investment value of US$8.4 billion. This included US$6.1 billion of non-recourse financing from 23 local, regional and international banks and financial institutions, along with a Euler Hermes tranche. Air Products was the nominated contractor and system integrator under a US$6.7 billion agreement, and holds an exclusive 30-year offtake agreement for the green ammonia.

Lesson: non-recourse debt at this scale rests on a long-term offtake and a single point of responsibility for delivery.

Ministerial City, Cotonou, Benin

In 2022, UK Export Finance supported the Government of Benin, represented by the Ministry of Economy and Finance, with a hybrid direct lending and buyer credit facility of about €121.2 million for a complex to house 21 ministries. Deutsche Bank acted as export credit agent bank, and a joint venture of Arabian Construction Company’s Benin and UK entities was the exporter.

Lesson: public buildings can be financed like infrastructure when the sovereign borrower, the export content and the contractor’s eligible entity line up.

Changfang and Xidao Offshore Wind, Taiwan

The 589 MW offshore wind project raised approximately US$3 billion from 25 international and Taiwanese banks and financial institutions and six export credit agencies, announced in February 2020. A 20-year power offtake agreement with the state-owned utility Taipower, and a local content plan approved by the government in December 2019, supported the financing.

Lesson: several agencies can share one project when the supply chain spans several countries and the revenue is contracted for the long term.

Talara Refinery Modernization, Peru

In November 2018, Petroperú signed a loan of up to US$1.3 billion guaranteed by CESCE, the Spanish export credit agency, with 13 international banks and a 10-year repayment term starting six months after the refinery entered operation. The financing supported the EPC contract led by Técnicas Reunidas.

Lesson: a state-owned company can finance a contractor’s scope through export credit when the contractor’s home agency is willing to lead.

What they have in common

  • A defined borrower and a credible repayment source: sovereign, corporate or contracted revenue.
  • An export package large enough for an agency to lead, and clear rules for the rest of the scope.
  • Delivery and financing designed together, with one party responsible for each major risk.
The structures differ. The discipline behind them does not.
