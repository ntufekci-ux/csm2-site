Where the contract is signed decides which agency can lead

Export credit agencies support exports from their own country. The contracting entity and the supply chain have to be planned with that in mind.

In brief
  • An agency supports exports from its own country, so the exporter’s location and the origin of supply drive eligibility.
  • When supply comes from several countries, agencies can share the risk through reinsurance or parallel cover.
  • Decide the lead agency, the contracting entity and the sourcing plan together, before the EPC package is fixed.

An export credit agency exists to support its country’s exports. UK Export Finance, for example, requires the exporter in a buyer credit to be carrying on business in the UK, and tests each transaction against its foreign content policy. Which company signs the EPC contract, and from where, is therefore part of the financing plan, not only a corporate or tax question.

What the agency looks at

  • Where the exporter is established and carries on business.
  • How much of the contract value is produced in the agency’s country, and how much comes from third countries or is spent locally.
  • Whether that content is documented well enough to be evidenced at each drawdown.
  • Country risk, and the agency’s appetite for the buyer’s market.

When supply comes from several countries

Large projects rarely source from a single country, and agencies have ways to share the risk. Under reinsurance, the agency of the main supplier covers the whole contract and reinsures the share supplied from another country with that country’s agency. Austria’s OeKB describes the main insurer as the agency of the country where the main supplier is domiciled, and allocates third-country content and local costs among the agencies in proportion to the contract value they generate. Under parallel insurance, each participant brings its own agency and the agencies coordinate. Germany’s federal export credit guarantees list reinsurance or joint insurance arrangements with a long list of other agencies.

Planning implications

  • Choose the lead agency first, based on where the largest share of eligible value will be created, then check reinsurance options for the rest.
  • If a contractor operates from more than one country, decide which entity signs the EPC contract with the financing route in mind.
  • Give the contracting entity real substance in its country: people, engineering, procurement and supply. Content rules look at where value is created, not only where a contract is signed.
  • Allow time. Reinsurance and parallel cover add parties, applications and approvals; OeKB notes that reinsurance premiums are payable up front.
The contracting entity is a financing decision as much as a corporate one.

Handled early, this is an advantage: a contractor with eligible entities in more than one export country can offer the project owner a choice of agencies and terms. Handled late, it is one of the most common reasons a financing plan has to be rebuilt.
